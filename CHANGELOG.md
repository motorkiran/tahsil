# Changelog

All notable changes to Tahsil are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/); until 1.0, minor versions may break compatibility.

## [0.1.0] - Unreleased

### Added

- The server foundation: configuration validated at startup, with Test and Live modes (Live mode refuses plain HTTP and the public development master key) and a `Tahsil-Mode: test` header and page banner in Test mode.
- PostgreSQL storage with automatic migrations, or the `migrate` command as a separate deployment step.
- Secrets encrypted with a master key that can be rotated: earlier keys stay configured for decryption, and the `reencrypt` command moves everything to the current key.
- Background workers that any number of instances share, with backoff and an attention report for items that keep failing, and an outbox that delivers side effects after their transaction commits.
- Hosted pages in Turkish and English with branding, a strict content security policy and a localized error page; API errors as RFC 9457 problem details with a `code` and a `traceId`.
- OpenTelemetry traces and metrics, `/health/live` and `/health/ready`, a container image (`dotnet publish -t:PublishContainer`) and a Compose file for local development.
- API keys (`ths_test_` and `ths_live_`, by the installation's mode) with permissions, an escalation guard, per-key rate limits and audit; `Idempotency-Key` on every `POST`, safe to retry even after a crash.
- The catalog: tax rates, meters, products and prices (one-time and recurring; per-unit, package and tiered; trials; lookup keys that can move to a new price), through the API and from a provisioning document applied at startup or through the API, with export.
- Provisioning documents also declare provider connections with the currencies routed to them, and webhook endpoints. In the startup file, credentials and webhook secrets are configuration references such as `${Iyzico:ApiKey}`, so secrets stay out of version control; documents sent through the API give them literally. Exports show each stored secret as a reference to configure where the document is applied. Webhook endpoints can be given a key and a secret of their own.
- Customers with external IDs, upsert by external ID, search, TCKN and VKN validation, encrypted tax IDs, deletion or anonymization, and data export for KVKK requests.
- Payment provider connections: credentials validated against each provider's fields, stored encrypted and never returned; a connection test; which connection serves each currency; Test mode takes sandbox connections and Live mode production ones, and a fresh Test mode installation starts with a simulator connection for TRY, USD and EUR.
- A built-in payment simulator for Test mode: test cards for successes, declines, 3D Secure, timeouts and slow or failing refunds, a card page that refuses any other card number, and settings that make it behave like providers with fewer capabilities.
- Invoices: drafts with one-time prices, free-form lines and pending invoice items, recalculated with tax per line; finalization with gapless yearly numbers in the billing time zone, a snapshot of the customer's billing details and the customer's balance applied; void and uncollectible invoices. Customer balances per currency, with adjustments and their history.
- Payments: invoices paid on a page with the customer present or off session with a saved card, recorded before the provider is called so an unanswered call is never lost; outcomes applied once whichever path brings them, late successes and charges of another amount set aside for an operator; cards saved from successful payments with the customer's consent, one record per provider token. `POST /invoices/{id}/pay` and the payments API.
- Payment workers: automatically collected invoices are charged to the customer's default card when due, and payments without a known outcome are reconciled with the provider on a backoff schedule, handed to an operator after seven days, or at once when the provider cannot be searched; customers' unfinished payments are canceled when their time runs out.
- Refunds: full or partial, never above the payment even when requested at once, as same-day voids where the provider wants them, refused early when a provider refunds parts only from the next day, settled later for providers that refund asynchronously, with a per-key daily limit; payments that succeed after nothing needs them are refunded automatically.
- Provider notifications at `/provider-notifications/{connectionId}`: verified by the connection's adapter, stored once per provider event with the raw body encrypted and kept 30 days, and applied by a worker that settles the payments and refunds Tahsil wrote ahead, ignores references that are not Tahsil's, and asks an operator after ten failed attempts. The simulator notifies itself with signed notifications, optionally late or twice.
- Webhooks: endpoints subscribe to Tahsil's event catalog and receive thin, signed events (Standard Webhooks) that are the audit events of their changes, retried nine times over about 45 hours, at most a few at once per endpoint, only to public addresses; rotated secrets keep signing beside the new one for 24 hours; the delivery log can be searched, redelivered and kept 30 days.
- Saved cards: list, default and removal (which deletes the token at the provider), expiry at the end of the card's month, and in Test mode an API that saves simulator test cards and scripts how they answer.
- Time travel in Test mode: `POST /api/v1/test/clock` moves the billing clock forward by an ISO 8601 duration or to an instant, processing everything that falls due on the way at its own instant, and returns a summary.
- The admin panel under `/admin`: sign-in through any OpenID Connect provider such as Kimlik (roles from the ID token, userinfo or access token; MFA required by default), or as the developer in Test mode during development; customers, products and prices, tax rates, API keys and the audit log.
- Admin screens for billing: provider connections (created from each provider's fields, tested, enabled or disabled) and currency routing; invoices (drafted on the customer's page, lines, finalize, pay with a saved card, void, mark uncollectible); payments with their history, filtered by status, connection or failure code, refunds and rulings on uncertain payments with the provider's evidence; webhook endpoints with their secrets, test events, delivery log and redelivery; the attention list, also counted on the home page; and in Test mode, time travel and simulator card behaviors. Customer pages show balances with adjustments, saved cards, invoices and payments. `GET /payments` filters by `failureCode`.
- Checkout sessions API: `payment` sessions sell one-time prices and `setup` sessions save a card, for an existing customer or one described to create later; the quote is computed with the invoicing pipeline when the session is created, the currency must be routed to a connection that can do what the session needs, and open sessions expire on time or on request with `checkout.expired`.
- The merchant's identity (`Tahsil:Merchant`) and the checkout's legal documents (`Tahsil:Checkout:LegalDocuments`): Liquid templates rendered with the session's data, or links; Turkish and English starter templates for the pre-information form and the distance sales contract, to be reviewed by the merchant's lawyer; a template whose content changes without a new version stops startup, so accepted versions stay provable.
- The hosted checkout page at `/checkout/{token}`, in the session's language: the order summary with taxes, the customer's details, the legal documents with their acceptance boxes, the consent to start at once, the final summary and a pay button that names the amount and waits for the required boxes; the provider's card fields embedded with their sources added to the page's security policy; the provider's return, a pending page that polls for the result, and another attempt after a failure. A successful payment completes the session once, whichever path brings it: a paid invoice of the quote for purchases, the saved card with its consent for setups, and `checkout.completed`.
- Email: SMTP delivery with MailKit (`Tahsil:Email`), Liquid templates in a branded layout with the merchant's identity, intents delivered through the outbox and sent once per occurrence; without SMTP, emails are recorded as skipped. The order confirmation goes out right after a checkout with the accepted legal documents attached, identical to the recorded copies. Compose's Mailpit receives development mail.
- Card testing protection on the checkout: payment attempts are limited per session, per network (an IPv4 address or an IPv6 /64) per hour and per customer's failures per day, before the provider is called; a decline spike on a connection raises an attention item and a metric, and an operator can pause new checkouts on the connection (`checkoutsPaused`, also in the admin panel) while saved cards keep being charged.
- The hosted invoice page at `/invoices/{token}` (`hostedInvoiceUrl` in the API and the admin panel): links that need no storage and stop working when `Tahsil:Invoices:LinkKeyVersion` changes; the seller and customer, lines with periods, a tax breakdown, totals, payments and refunds, in the customer's language with a print layout for saving as PDF; titles that say what the document is (a payment request, or a receipt once paid) and that it is not a tax invoice until a legal invoice is recorded; card payment through the currency's connection and bank transfer instructions per currency.
- The iyzico adapter: Checkout Form payments with 3D Secure and card storage (with the card's bank and expiry), renewals of stored cards without 3D Secure, lookups through reporting, same-day cancels and later refunds, card removal, and V3-signed notifications as triggers for reconciliation; IYZWSv2 signing and response signatures are verified, and iyzico's error groups map to Tahsil's failure codes. Adapters can now report that a provider refused to start a payment.
- A provider conformance suite that runs the adapter contract against the simulator in every build and against iyzico's sandbox when its keys are set; an operator guide for iyzico (`docs/guides/providers/iyzico.md`); the admin panel shows each connection's notification URL.
- A failure injection suite as the payments core's exit check: timeouts before and after the provider charged, a process killed between writing a payment ahead and recording it, repeated, out-of-order and simultaneous outcomes, late successes, amount mismatches, slow and failing refunds and forged notifications, each followed through to the webhooks applications receive; and every event of the catalog checked against the scenario that emits it.
- Billing periods computed in the billing time zone from the subscription's anchor: month ends kept (31 January renews on 28 February, then 31 March), no drift over years, and daylight saving gaps and overlaps resolved the same way every time; proration of plan and quantity changes by the remaining share of the period, to the second.
- Subscriptions in storage: items of recurring prices that share a currency and an interval, the statuses `incomplete`, `trialing`, `active`, `past_due`, `canceled` and `incomplete_expired`, trials, billing cycle anchors with a shorter first period, scheduled cancellations, and the time each subscription is due for its next cycle.
- Subscriptions through the API (`POST /api/v1/subscriptions`, list and get): with a trial they start trialing without a card; otherwise the first invoice is finalized at once and charged to the card during the request (`incomplete` when declined, with the invoice's page to pay it within a day, which then activates the subscription) or sent for `send_invoice`; a later billing cycle anchor bills a shorter first period; `subscription.created` and `subscription.updated` events.
- The subscription engine: renewals on time and period by period after downtime, trial ends (canceled without a card unless invoicing was agreed), scheduled cancellations and the expiry of unpaid incomplete subscriptions; cycle invoices that stay drafts for `Tahsil:Billing:DraftPeriod` (one hour by default) while items for the subscription can join them, then finalize themselves and are charged to the subscription's card or the customer's default; a failed renewal charge makes the subscription `past_due` until its invoices are paid. Time travel steps through all of it; `subscription.renewed` and `subscription.canceled` events.
- Subscription changes (`PATCH /api/v1/subscriptions/{id}`): new items now, prorated to the second and invoiced at once (`always_invoice`), left for the next renewal (`create_prorations`) or not prorated (`none`), or at a later renewal; another billing interval restarts the period; downgrades become customer credit that later invoices spend; trials can be extended or ended now; the card, the collection method and metadata change too, guarded by `If-Match`. Higher prices for individual customers must be scheduled at least `Tahsil:Subscriptions:PriceIncreaseNotice` (30 days) ahead. Previews of a change (`POST /api/v1/subscriptions/{id}/preview`) and of the next renewal (`GET /api/v1/invoices/upcoming?subscription=`) match the invoices later created.
- Cancellation (`POST /api/v1/subscriptions/{id}/cancel`): at the period end, at a time within the period, or now with a final invoice of the pending items and, if asked, a refund of the unused share of the period (capped at what its payments can still refund) or of the last payment; `resume` takes back a scheduled cancellation.
- Admin screens for subscriptions: a list with status and product filters, and a page per subscription with its items, periods, scheduled change and invoices, to change the plan after a preview, cancel in each mode, resume, move or end a trial, and retry a past due payment; customer pages list their subscriptions. Invoices can be listed by subscription.
- A subscription exit scenario as the billing engine's check: twelve months of renewals at month ends with an upgrade, a downgrade and a cancellation, every invoice compared line by line, and trials with and without cards.
- Dunning: declines that may pass are retried 1, 3, 5 and 7 days after the first failure (`Tahsil:Dunning:RetrySchedule`); cards that cannot work and payments that need 3D Secure wait for the customer (`invoice.action_required`) and are tried at once when a new card is given; the first customer failure makes the subscription past due (`subscription.past_due`); when the schedule runs out unpaid, the subscription is canceled for non-payment and the invoice left open, marked uncollectible or voided as configured. The merchant's and provider's failures never count against the customer: they are retried on a technical backoff and reported to operators after a day. Several open invoices of a subscription are collected oldest first.
- Invoices sent for payment: emailed with their hosted page link and bank transfer details when issued, reminded of 3 days before the due date, on it, and 3 and 7 days after (`Tahsil:Invoices:Reminders`), each once; when the due date passes unpaid, `invoice.overdue` is sent and the subscription is past due, and dunning's final action follows a day after the last reminder. `POST /api/v1/invoices/{id}/send` emails an invoice again, at most once every ten minutes.
- Bank transfers, cash and other payments received outside Tahsil are recorded against an invoice (`POST /api/v1/invoices/{id}/pay` with `outOfBand`), in full or in part but never above what is left, and can be refunded as a record of money returned outside Tahsil.
- Customer emails in Turkish and English for receipts, failed payments, payments the bank wants confirmed, the last reminder before cancellation, cancellations, refunds and price increases, each sent once; templates can be replaced from `Tahsil:Notifications:TemplatesPath` (checked at startup). Operators get an email for each item that needs attention and a daily digest of those still open (`Tahsil:Notifications:OperatorEmail`).
- Reminders, each once: a trial's end 3 days ahead (for card trials, with the first charge's amount and date, and not possible to turn off), a yearly renewal 14 days ahead (configurable per interval), and a card that pays a subscription 30 days before it expires; with the `subscription.trial_will_end`, `subscription.will_renew` and `payment_method.will_expire` events.
- Admin screens for collection: invoices record bank transfers, cash and other payments in full or in part, and invoices sent for payment can be emailed again; the invoice list filters those in dunning or overdue (also `dunning` and `overdue` on `GET /api/v1/invoices`), and invoices show since when they are in dunning and when the final action comes (`dunningStartedAt`, `dunningDeadlineAt`).
- A collection exit scenario as the dunning and notification check, with email sent over SMTP to Mailpit: a failed renewal recovered by another card, an unrecovered one canceled on schedule, the merchant's failures never canceling a customer even past the deadline, and a company paying its invoice by bank transfer, each customer receiving their emails once and in their language.
- Coupons and promotion codes (`/api/v1/coupons`, `/api/v1/promotion-codes`): a percentage or an amount in one currency off, once, for 1 to 120 cycles or forever, optionally for some products only, with a redemption limit and a last redemption time; the discount, duration and products never change, and coupons are never deleted. Codes of 3 to 40 letters, digits, `_` or `-` are stored in uppercase and unique ignoring case, may expire, have a limit, be for first purchases only, need a minimum subtotal or be for one customer, and can be deactivated and reactivated. Both come with provisioning (`coupons` with their `promotionCodes`), export, audit and admin screens.
- Discounts: subscriptions take a `coupon` or `promotionCode` when created or patched (`coupon: null` or `DELETE /api/v1/subscriptions/{id}/discount` removes it, and a new one replaces it), and manual invoices a `coupon` while they are drafts. Every invoice of a subscription created while its discount lasts is discounted, prorations included; `once` ends after the first invoice, `repeating` after its number of renewal invoices, and `forever` when removed; an open draft drops a discount that ended. Percentages discount each line with its sign, amounts are shared by the positive lines in their currency only, and coupons for some products discount only their lines. Redemptions are counted under row locks, so limits hold under concurrency, and refusals name the reason: archived, expired, used up, inactive, for another customer or first purchases only, below the minimum, in another currency or for other products. Invoices list their discounts by name (`discounts`), subscriptions show theirs (`discount`), and so does the admin panel.
- Subscription checkout (`mode: subscription`): sessions sell recurring prices of one interval, with one-time prices on the first invoice, and complete into an active subscription whose first invoice the payment paid, with the card saved as its payment method under a consent that states the amount, how often, the first automatic charge and that it can be canceled. A trial collects no card by default and starts when the form is submitted, without a payment; `subscription.paymentMethodCollection: always` saves a card for it without charging. When the customer does not let the provider save the card, the subscription is created anyway and the confirmation email asks for a card before the renewal; when the connection cannot save cards at all, the subscription is collected by invoices (`send_invoice`, due on the day by default) and trials with a card are refused. The quote also shows what each renewal bills (`quote.recurring`), sessions report their `subscription` terms and `subscriptionId`, and subscriptions sent as invoices may now be due in 0 days. The simulator can mimic a provider that saves no cards (`savesCards: none`).
- Discounts in checkout: sessions take a `coupon` or `promotionCode` from the application, and with `allowPromotionCodes` the page has a field where the customer enters a code. The quote, the summary and the pay button follow at once, with the discount named as `Launch discount (−50%, 3 months)`; the redemption is counted when the session completes, on the purchase's invoice or on the subscription. A code that cannot be used is refused without saying why, so the page does not reveal which codes exist, and guessing is limited to 10 codes per session and 30 per hour per network. A code for first purchases or for another customer is taken off, with the new total shown, when the form reveals who is buying. Quotes are now computed by the same calculation as invoices.
- The customer portal, first part: `POST /api/v1/portal-sessions` opens the portal for a customer the application has authenticated, at a link that works for that customer alone until it expires (`Tahsil:Portal:SessionLifetime`, an hour by default) and can name a task to open on (`flow`). The portal's overview at `/portal/{token}` shows the customer's subscriptions with what their next renewal bills, saved cards, invoices with their pages, and billing details, in their language, each section a feature that can be turned off (`Tahsil:Portal:Features`). An expired link answers "not found" on a page that leads back to the application. The emails about a trial ending, a failed payment, an expiring card and a price change now carry a portal link that works for a week (`Tahsil:Portal:EmailLinkLifetime`). Changing plans, cards and cancelling in the portal come next.
- Plan changes and cards in the portal. A customer switches among the prices of `Tahsil:Portal:Products` and, within `Tahsil:Portal:QuantityLimits`, changes the quantity: the page shows each price with its period and amount including tax, then what the change charges or credits now and the next renewal, and the button names the obligation ("Pay ₺150.00 and upgrade"). An upgrade takes effect at once and is charged to the card, or paid on the invoice's page when the card fails; a downgrade waits for the period's end unless `Tahsil:Portal:DowngradeTiming` is `now`. The change is recorded with the button and amounts shown. On the payment method page a customer adds a card in the provider's own fields, with their consent to keep it; it becomes the default for them and for the subscriptions that used the old one, and invoices that waited for a card are tried at once. They can also make another card the default and remove the others; with a provider that cannot save a card without a payment, the page sends them to their open invoice instead. Sessions with a `flow` now open on its page and return to the application when the task is done.
- Cancelling, withdrawal and billing details in the portal. A customer ends a subscription with one optional question and one confirmation: individuals choose between the end of the period (or of the trial) and now with the unused time refunded; companies get the period's end, or both with `Tahsil:Portal:CompanyCancellation: both`. With the `withdrawal` feature, an individual who did not agree at checkout to the service starting at once may withdraw within 14 days, and every payment of the subscription is refunded (`refund: all_payments` in the API too). A configured `Tahsil:Portal:RetentionCoupon` is offered before the cancellation is confirmed. A cancellation scheduled for the period's end can be taken back. Each choice is recorded as shown and confirmed by email: the cancellation email now also goes out when the cancellation is scheduled, plan changes send `subscription_updated` with what was charged or credited and the next renewal, and a withdrawal sends `withdrawal_confirmed`. Customers keep their name, address, phone, email and tax details up to date on the billing details page; finalized invoices keep the details they were issued with.
- Promotion codes on plan changes in the portal, with the `promotion_codes` feature: the preview shows the discounted amounts, the code is redeemed with the change, and attempts are limited like the checkout's. `POST /subscriptions/{id}/preview` takes a `coupon` or `promotionCode` and previews the discount without redeeming it.
- The M6 exit scenario: a customer redeems a code at checkout, upgrades, gives a new card that pays the renewal and cancels with a refund, and every invoice, refund and email along the way is checked.
- The Kimlik connector's settings (`Tahsil:Kimlik`), checked at startup when it is enabled. With it, a product's `kimlikPlan` must name a base plan open in Kimlik (`product.unknown_kimlik_plan`), and a customer's `kimlikSubscriber`, also in a checkout's customer details, an existing Kimlik user or organization (`customer.unknown_kimlik_subscriber`), whenever Kimlik can be reached. A customer's Kimlik link no longer changes while they have a subscription to a product with a Kimlik plan (`customer.kimlik_link_locked`).
